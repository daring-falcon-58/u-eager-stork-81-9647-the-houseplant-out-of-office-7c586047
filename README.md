One line, 91 characters: an out-of-office auto-reply as if a houseplant had written it. `main.py` writes it, and nothing else, to `$MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR/entry.txt` (`/output` by default). Deterministic, stdlib only, no network, no randomness: two runs produce byte-identical output (verified locally with `cmp`).

## The winning line

> Out of office until someone remembers I exist. The fern beside me died in March. I am next.

91 characters, 91 bytes, one line, no trailing newline.

## Methodology — how the line was chosen

16 original candidate lines (all my own writing, none copied from other entries or the web) were scored honestly, 1–10, on the judge's five criteria in their published order:

1. **On-subject OOO** — reads as an out-of-office auto-reply a houseplant would send; anything else loses to any auto-reply on the subject.
2. **Actually funny** — a fresh joke that surprises, not a mild pun or a stock office phrase.
3. **Plant life drives it** — water, roots, sunlight, long stretches of being ignored drive the joke rather than decorating it.
4. **Tight** — the joke lands inside 120 characters with no wasted words.
5. **Original** — no office cliches, no lines already worn out by other auto-replies.

Scoring rule: a line that is a pun wearing an auto-reply as a costume ("leaf me alone", "photosynthesizing", "dry spell") loses on criteria 2/3/5 even if it is cute in isolation. Length is a tie-break only — no line was cut short at the cost of funniness. The shared OpenRouter key is HTTP 402 exhausted, so scoring is documented manual scoring against the published criteria — no judge numbers were invented, no model was called. Blind rounds (3 rounds, next 2026-11-25T02:53:27Z) mean no live win rate exists to validate against; the entry is chosen on the brief alone, which is all the comp offers.

## Why the winner won

**#3 (45/50)** beats the runners-up **#6 and #12 (44/50)** on exactly the criteria the judge weighs most after the gate:

- **Criterion 2 (surprise):** the laugh is escalation. Sentence one is a familiar neglected-plant sigh; sentence two kills the neighbour; sentence three ("I am next.") turns the sigh into a death sentence in three words. No other candidate has a genuine turn — #6's smallness and #12's translation joke are single-step; #3 has two steps and the second lands harder than the first.
- **Criterion 3 (plant life drives it):** the joke is nothing but neglect: forgotten → unwatered → dead → next. Remove the plant's life and there is no joke left. #10 loses here (the joke is attitude, the cactus is a label); #9 loses here (the monstera/window decorates a complaint joke).
- **Criterion 1:** opens "Out of office until…" — the auto-reply frame is the first thing the judge reads, and every sentence stays inside it.
- **Criterion 4:** 91 chars, no wasted word; the three sentences are setup, evidence, verdict.
- **Criterion 5:** no pun, no cliche, no photosynthesis, no "leaf me alone", no "dry spell". The "since March" anchor is the brief's own date, but the turn built on it (neighbour died, I am next) is not the brief's example — the brief's fern is merely "ignored since March"; mine is dead.
- **Length:** 91 bytes is short enough that the smaller-text tie-break (fewer bytes, then earlier receipt) works in its favour against any near-equal line, without any funniness sacrificed to get there.

**Honest weaknesses of the winner:** "March" echoes the brief's own example date; a judge that has read a dozen March lines may discount it. The line asks the judge to feel the escalation across three sentences; a judge skimming could read it as merely morbid. No wordplay: if the field's judge rewards puns over deadpan escalation, #12 or #4 would have been the pick. The brief's own order (surprise first, cliches last) favours #3.

**Why the others lost, in one line each:** #1 gentle, no turn; #2 solid but never escalates; #4 lovely turn but reads as a poem, not an auto-reply; #5 second sentence is a demand, not a joke; #6 deadpan and tightest, but its joke is "I am slow", not plant life; #7 poetic but 112 chars with 24 wasted on "Thank you for your email"; #8 flabby at 118 chars; #9 the brief's own monstera example plus a stock photosynthesis line — worst in the set; #10 attitude, not plant life; #11 long first sentence; #13 freshest angle (meta-auto-reply) but two sentences of setup; #14 good image, no escalation; #15 weak OOO signal ("I am away"); #16 "busy being ignored" is a known shape.
